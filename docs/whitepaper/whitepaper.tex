\begin{abstract}
Horizon verifies an inequality between an amount and a per-case limit,
attested by two distinct roles, before recording a synthetic authorization.
It uses Pedersen over Ristretto, Bulletproofs and Ed25519 signatures.
This text provides conditional derivations, separates confidentiality,
authenticity and recovery, and supplies executable arithmetic checks.
It is neither a machine-checked formal proof, a cryptographic audit, a new
primitive nor banking qualification. Octave tests do not prove cryptographic
security. The profile is not post-quantum.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose, scope and correspondence with the code}
The \code{horizon-range-v1} profile is in
\code{labs/horizon/proof/src/lib.rs}. The demonstration effect is
\code{record_claim_authorization}, in a local SQLite registry, not a payment.
The intended economic use case is delegated insurance claim authorization;
no paying demand, automatic compliance or superiority over Vega is established.

The library verifies a \emph{cryptographic statement}. The controller
\code{packages/horizon-demo/src/store.ts} adds local expectations,
nonce consumption, business deduplication and state management. The publication
SDK \code{packages/proofkit-sdk} remains experimental:
an interface does not replace any of these assumptions.

\section{Parameters and issuance contract}
Let $\mathbb G$ be the Ristretto group of prime order
\begin{align*}
q&=2^{252}+27742317777372353535851937790883648493.
\end{align*}
Canonical encodings and the prime-order group are specified by RFC~9496
\cite{rfc9496}. $G,H\in\mathbb G$ are the fixed bases from
\code{PedersenGens::default()}. The prover does not choose them. Set $N=2^{32}$.

Two authorities produce $A=\Com(a,r_a)=aG+r_aH$ and
$C=\Com(c,r_c)=cG+r_cH$. The contract requires nonnegative integers
representable as \code{u64}, the same EUR currency and the same exponent 2.
Signatures cover profile, role, authority, tenant, subject, currency, exponent,
version, holder key, period and commitment. Trust keys are pinned;
the two authority identifiers and keys must be distinct.

\begin{assumption}[Issuance and joining]
Each authority computes or checks its commitment opening from its facts.
It does not blindly sign a point submitted by the holder.
The amount authority does not necessarily know the limit, and vice versa.
Both openings reach the prover, which knows both values.
Shared metadata actually refers to the same case and business version.
The controller independently associates that subject with the target object.
\end{assumption}
Two different keys do not prove organizational independence, quality of
expertise or absence of collusion. Schema version 1 of the prototype is not
an insurance claims history registry. A signature attests issuance under a key,
not the truth of the signed fact.

\section{Relation and conditional derivations}
\subsection{Commitment: confidentiality and binding}
\begin{lemma}[Hiding in the ideal model]
For $H\ne0$ and $r$ uniform in $\Zq$, the distribution of $vG+rH$ is uniform
in $\mathbb G$ for any fixed value $v$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Multiplication $r\mapsto rH$ is a bijection because $q$ is prime
and $H\ne0$. Translation by $vG$ preserves uniformity. Commitment
distributions are therefore equal for any two values.
\end{proof}
This result concerns the commitment alone. In the code, the quality of the
random generator and conversion to scalars remains an implementation assumption.
It does not justify masks that are reused or selected based on the secret.

\begin{lemma}[Computational binding]
Producing two openings $(v,r)\ne(v',r')$ of the same commitment with
$v\ne v'\pmod q$ makes it possible to compute $\log_G H$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equality gives $(v-v')G=(r'-r)H$. If $r'=r$, then $v=v'\pmod q$,
a contradiction. Thus $(r'-r)^{-1}$ exists in $\Zq$ and
$\log_G H=(v-v')(r'-r)^{-1}\pmod q$.
\end{proof}
Confidentiality is information-theoretic in the ideal model; binding depends
on the discrete logarithm and bases with no known relation
\cite{pedersen1992,daleknotes}. No reduced numerical experiment validates
this assumption for Ristretto.

\subsection{The statement actually proved}
The derived commitments are
\[
 X=A-G,\qquad D=C-A.
\]
The prover invokes an aggregated range proof for two 32-bit values
\cite{bulletproofs,dalek5}, with openings
\[
 x=a-1,\quad d=c-a,\quad r_X=r_a,\quad r_D=r_c-r_a\pmod q.
\]
The opening relation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relation}
\exists x,d\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\},\ r_X,r_D\in\Zq:
 X=xG+r_XH,\quad D=dG+r_DH.
\end{equation}
\textbf{A necessary distinction:} since $H$ generates the group, every point
mathematically has an opening to any value. A mere existential membership
statement is therefore insufficient to bind a business value. The argument
uses the \emph{argument of knowledge} of openings and computational binding
to the openings actually attested.

\begin{assumption}[Inherited primitive]
The noninteractive Bulletproofs protocol used provides completeness,
computational knowledge of openings and confidentiality appropriate to its
security model and Fiat--Shamir transcripts.
Its implementation, parameters, randomness and transcript analysis match
that protocol. The corrected paper is the reference; this dossier does not
redo its reduction or audit the library.
\end{assumption}
The ePrint paper reports a correction to its Fiat--Shamir section in 2022
\cite{bulletproofs}. This history rules out treating a security citation
as an automatic proof for every software adaptation.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional completeness]
If signatures and metadata are valid, the policy permits the mode,
the challenge is the expected one and has not expired, and
$1\le a\le N$, $a\le c<a+N$, then an honest prover constructs both
derived commitments with admissible values. Under the primitive's
completeness and in the absence of execution errors, its proof is accepted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$x=a-1\in[0,N-1]$ and $d=c-a\in[0,N-1]$. By linearity,
$X=(a-1)G+r_aH$ and $D=(c-a)G+(r_c-r_a)H$.
These are exactly the inputs expected by the verifier.
It tests the other preconditions separately.
\end{proof}
This completeness guarantees neither network availability, absence of
revocation after computation nor success of a business effect.

\begin{theorem}[Integer bounds and absence of wraparound]\label{th:bounds}
Assume extraction of the openings in \eqref{eq:relation}, binding of the
commitments and issuance in the \code{u64} integer domain. Then the
attested values satisfy
\[
1\le a\le2^{32},\qquad 0\le c-a<2^{32},\qquad c\le2^{33}-1.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The extracted openings give an opening of $A$ to $a'=x+1$ with
mask $r_X$, and of $C$ to $c'=x+1+d$ with mask $r_X+r_D$.
They satisfy $1\le a'\le N$ and $a'\le c'\le2N-1$.
If $a'$ or $c'$ differ modulo $q$ from the attested values, this
violates binding. Otherwise $a=a'\pmod q$ and $c=c'\pmod q$.
The attested values lie between 0 and $2^{64}-1<q$, and the extracted
values between 0 and $2^{33}-1<q$. Two congruent integers in $[0,q)$
are equal; the bounds are therefore integer bounds, not merely modular ones.
\end{proof}
The limit may exceed $2^{32}-1$: \code{u64} is needed. The maximum
$8\,589\,934\,591$ cents equals $85\,899\,345.91$ EUR, without any claim
that this maximum suits an insurance contract.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional computational soundness]
Conditional on honest authorities for issuance, binding, the inherited
argument of knowledge, signature unforgeability and a faithful implementation,
accepting an attested value that violates the bounds in
Proposition~\ref{th:bounds} requires breaking at least one of these assumptions
or the context-binding checks.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A signature on an unissued attestation is a forgery.
For issued attestations, extraction and the previous proposition impose
the bounds. An incorrect context association requires either an erroneous
trusted input, an encoding/comparison error or a break in the mechanism
that authenticates that context.
\end{proof}
A failure bound can be organized as a sum of forgery, collision,
extraction-failure and binding-break events.
Without a complete reduction and oracle model, this dossier supplies neither
reduction constants nor a numerical security probability. The 500 rejections
in a campaign are not an estimate of soundness.

\section{Composition with signatures and challenge}
The expected challenge is issued and stored by the controller. It contains
tenant, client, audience, action, operation, object, subject, policy and epoch,
trust epoch, 32-byte nonce, timestamps and method. Equality covers
\emph{all} these fields before authorization. The agent does not supply
trust roots. Encodings use typed JSON in declaration order with separate
domains; this is not a promise of universal canonical JSON.

\begin{theorem}[Presentation binding]
Assume Ed25519 is unforgeable for honest keys, SHA-256 is collision-resistant,
domains are distinct and encoding is injective over the admitted types.
A presentation accepted under an independent challenge is bound to the
attestation pair, payload and challenge by the holder key.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The holder signature covers the digest of the profile, attestations,
challenge and payload tuple, itself encoded under a separate domain.
Changing any of these objects without obtaining a new signature requires
a collision or forgery. An input with another challenge is rejected by
equality with the expected value. Authority signatures cover each
attestation body separately.
\end{proof}
RFC~8032 describes EdDSA \cite{rfc8032}; the code uses strict verification
and rejects weak keys. A compromised key or malicious authority lies
outside the assumption, and two identical presentations can still be
replayed without consumption state.

\paragraph{Mode A.} The entire challenge is also added to the Merlin
transcript. The range proof is recomputed for each presentation. This is
cryptographic context binding, not a clock or proof of instantaneous
source truth.
\paragraph{Mode B.} The range proof is prepared over immutable attestations
and retained. A new holder signature covers the challenge and the exact
proof. This attests fresh authorization by that key over a historical proof,
\emph{not} fresh knowledge of the witness. The current verifier checks the
entire proof on every call.
\paragraph{Policy C.} If confidentiality is not required, the selector
chooses signed disclosure. Otherwise, it chooses B if reuse is permitted
and A if not. Cryptographic rejection never triggers a fallback.
Method authorization remains independently verified.

\section{Replay prevention and transaction boundary}
The controller's operation identity is the hash of the tenant, audience,
action and object tuple. It does not contain the nonce. The guarantee is local
to this deduplication domain; multiple audiences or independent databases do
not automatically share a global business identity.

\begin{assumption}[Storage and target model]
Transactions are serialized and atomic. Committed states persist without
backup rollback, administrative deletion or corruption.
All effect paths pass through the same controller, the same stable business
identity and the same database. The \code{Verified} result comes from the
trusted cryptographic worker, never from a forged HTTP field.
The clock and trust roots are controlled by the verifier.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[At most one local effect per business identity]
Under the preceding assumption, the controller produces at most one effect
row for an operation identity, including with multiple nonces, concurrency,
a crash after reservation or an ambiguous response.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Initially, no operation exists. A transaction validates the current context,
consumes the nonce and inserts the operation under a unique primary key.
Any subsequent reservation with that key is rejected. The target transaction
requires the reserved state, rechecks deadlines and epochs, then inserts the
effect under the same primary key and completes the operation. Failure of
this recheck preserves a rejected operation; an ambiguous response preserves
an unknown state. None of these states is deleted to retry.
Reconciliation queries existing effects and does not redispatch.
By induction over transitions, the number of effects remains at most one.
\end{proof}
SQLite provides transaction mechanisms under its documented assumptions
\cite{sqlite,sqliteatomic}. The proof is an abstract invariant, not a theorem
verified over the engine, filesystem or TypeScript code.
It proves neither that an effect will eventually occur nor atomicity over
a remote API. A crash after reservation may leave an operation blocked.
The chained log detects certain modifications if a root is known, but an
administrator able to rewrite the entire database is not excluded by a
mere local hash.

Temporal validity is the intersection of intervals:
\[
t_{\min}=\max(t_{\mathrm{challenge}},t_A,t_C),\qquad
t_{\max}=\min(e_{\mathrm{challenge}},e_A,e_C),\qquad
t_{\min}\le t<t_{\max}.
\]
Revocations and epochs are rechecked after the cryptographic wait and before
the effect. This bounds a snapshot's validity without proving the freshness
of an external system unavailable during a crisis.

\section{Leakage and business counterexamples}
Values and openings are private from the verifier in A/B under the proof's
assumptions. Authorities know their own values, the prover knows both, and
disclosed mode reveals them all.
Commitments, signatures, keys, opaque case identifier, tenant, roles,
timestamps, policy and decision are public. Fixed commitments make sessions
linkable; rerandomizing the proof alone does not make them anonymous.
The decision reveals at least the inequality and bounds. Adaptive policies,
auxiliary information and timing channels may reveal more; no system-level
noninterference is demonstrated.

These examples do not bypass existing controls; they delimit the statement.
Complete insurance claim logic must specify exclusions, deductibles, taxes,
coinsurance, coverage dates and reserves. No circuit becomes perfect
merely by increasing its speed.

\section{CPU, GPU and Vega measurements, and quantum assumptions}
Measurements retained in \code{docs/horizon/BENCHMARK_RESULTS.json}
use 500 observations after 30 warmups. Linux cryptographic functions cover
five cases; quantiles use the upper rank. IID independence of observations
is not established, and confidence intervals are conditional on it.
The same fixed public parameters are initialized once per process with
\code{OnceLock}: no proof, witness, verdict or randomness is cached there.
Mode B retains its prepared proof separately, in accordance with its semantics.

The proof size here is $32(2\log_2(2\cdot32)+9)=672$ bytes for the
aggregated format used. It does not include public commitments or
authorizations. The observed p95 reduction from retaining parameters is
15.75\% for A and 44.97\% for B verification; no external record follows.

Vega addresses credential presentations and reusable preparation
\cite{vega,vegacode}. The public repository's SHA-256 benchmark is not the
full application circuit behind the paper's 92/23 ms figures. Horizon instead
requires adapted issuance over commitments and leaves signatures and metadata
public. Relations, leakage models and phases differ: no speed ratio supports
concluding that one beats the other on a shared task.

The GPU dossier is retained separately in \code{docs/experiments/gpu/}.
A CUDA/RISC Zero attempt is not a GPU port of this Bulletproofs relation.
This text attributes no GPU speedup without a reproduced result, a statement
of the relation, size, host/device transfer, preparation, independent
verification and exact hardware. CUDA unavailability is a negative feasibility
result, not a speed measurement. GPU figures are therefore not interpolated
from CPU figures.

Finally, GPU acceleration and quantum resistance are different axes.
Efficient discrete-logarithm computation would compromise binding and elliptic
curve signatures; Shor's algorithm motivates this distinction \cite{shor}.
ML-DSA and SLH-DSA are post-quantum signature standards
\cite{fips204,fips205}. Replacing Ed25519 with one of them would not make
Pedersen/Bulletproofs post-quantum. Using SHA-256 or SHA-3 in a transcript does
not turn an elliptic curve protocol into an entirely hash-based proof.
Migration requires another proof relation, a composition analysis and
new benchmarks.

\section{Reproducible checks and limits of the evidence}
\code{math/horizon_checks.m} enumerates integer pairs in a reduced domain
for 1 to 6 bits, including negative values; checks 32-bit boundaries using
\code{uint64}; reconstructs $q$ through decimal strings; and explores all
reachable states of a finite reservation model with two nonces and one
operation. Decimal arithmetic functions handle only small digits, never
$q$ in floating point. No curve, signature or Bulletproofs implementation
is recreated in MATLAB/Octave.

The JSON result explicitly states runtime, version, UTC date and limitations.
The container pins the base digest, and the dossier retains the built image
identifier and package versions. The Dockerfile still depends on the package
repository at rebuild time: this is not a promise of bit-for-bit reproducible
builds. Execution has no network after building. MATLAB requires a separate
runtime/license: an Octave success is not presented as a MATLAB execution.

Existing Rust and Node adversarial tests remain separate experimental evidence.
The reduced model replaces neither verification of a real cryptographic proof
by an independent process, a model checker applied to the code nor an independent
security review. The PDF compilation log and page inspection attest only to
the document's production.

\section{Technical decision}
The defensible result is a feasibility laboratory: a narrow relation, explicit
assumptions, real proofs and a local controller that rejects before its effect.
Production security still requires qualified authorities, key management,
revocation, isolation, audit, a remote recovery model and validation of the
economic need. Cloud deployments remain network/IAM configuration examples,
not operational certification. No theorem presented here implies compliance,
absence of competition or universally correct business logic.
